# ProcessEd Convergence Report V2
Date: 2026-04-01  
Audience: Founders, investors, institutional buyers, and strategic partners  
Primary wedge: SME-first (locked)

## Executive Summary

Public procurement is entering a new software cycle. The center of gravity is shifting from notice discovery to decision intelligence because three forces are converging at once:

1. Public procurement data infrastructure is becoming more structured and reusable (UK central digital platform + enhanced Find a Tender, EU eForms/TED evolution).
2. Buyers and suppliers need higher-confidence bid/no-bid decisions instead of larger alert volumes.
3. Compliance, integrity, and supplier verification obligations are expanding (debarment, performance notices, ownership scrutiny, direct marketing controls).

This creates a category gap: existing products are strong in one layer but weak at the intersection. Tender tools surface opportunities but rarely produce explainable decisions. Risk/compliance tools verify entities but often ignore opportunity workflow. Investigative tools map networks but are not built for recurring commercial operations.

ProcessEd can occupy this gap by positioning as a decision-grade procurement intelligence layer:

- Best entry wedge: SME bid decision engine (fastest revenue, shortest cycle).
- Second wedge: institutional supplier-risk and procurement assurance intelligence (higher ACV).
- Moat path: entity-linked procurement graph plus decision provenance, not alert volume.

The opportunity is structurally attractive because the same data/decision spine can serve four buyer lenses at once:

1. SME contractor growth and bid efficiency.
2. Legal, investigative, and anti-corruption workflows.
3. Institutional supplier-risk and accountability operations.
4. Long-term investable data infrastructure (API + graph intelligence).

The category winner is unlikely to be "best tender alerts." The winner is likely to be the platform with the most trusted procurement truth graph, best explainability, and strongest decision-to-outcome feedback loop.

## 2) Four-Lens Market Landscape

### Lens A: Commercial B2B product (SME contractors)

Core pain:
- Too many irrelevant opportunities.
- High bid effort on low-probability contracts.
- Weak/no formal decision process.

What they buy:
- Fewer, better opportunities.
- Fast go/no-go confidence.
- Operational certainty and time savings.

### Lens B: Fraud detection and public accountability

Core buyers:
- Public law teams, investigative media, anti-corruption units, oversight bodies.

Core pain:
- Entity linkage across suppliers, awards, and ownership is manual.
- No unified procurement-to-risk trace in one place.

What they buy:
- Evidence-ready anomaly detection.
- Repeat winner patterns, pricing anomalies, exclusion-risk overlays.
- Case-ready timeline reconstruction.

### Lens C: Investable technology asset

What investors care about:
- Repeatability and low marginal delivery cost.
- Defensible data/decision moat.
- Expansion from SME ACV to institutional ACV without replacing architecture.

### Lens D: Public-sector / institutional intelligence product

Core buyers:
- Contracting authorities, central commercial functions, audit/risk teams.

Core pain:
- Siloed visibility and late issue detection.
- Limited supplier-level risk context during pipeline and award phases.

What they buy:
- Supplier-risk overlays.
- Cross-agency benchmarking.
- Early warning intelligence tied to procurement events.

## 3) Market Sizing: Three Quantified Views

### View 1: Infrastructure gravity (top-down)

- OECD procurement share: 12.7% of GDP (OECD), 14.8% (OECD-EU).  
- World Bank procurement range: 13% to 20% of GDP; global spend around $9.5T.
- UK procurement context references around GBP 300B to GBP 400B annual scale under current policy framing.  
Sources: [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/government-at-a-glance-2025_0efd0bcd-en/full-report/size-of-public-procurement_6979cd47.html), [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2020/03/23/global-public-procurement-database-share-compare-improve), [UK announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-public-procurement-rules-to-drive-growth-opportunities-for-small-businesses-and-exclude-suppliers-that-fail-to-deliver)

Sizing logic:
- Procurement software does not need to capture spend; it captures decision influence around spend.

### View 2: Integrity loss/exposure (top-down risk pool)

- World Bank indicates 10% to 25% of contract value can be lost to corruption in vulnerable contexts.
- Applied globally, this implies a broad exposure range of roughly $0.95T to $2.4T.
- Applied illustratively to UK GBP 400B context: GBP 40B to GBP 100B equivalent exposure band (illustrative, not recoverable TAM).

Sizing logic:
- Integrity tools monetize prevention, assurance, investigation, and compliance budgets, not loss recovery directly.

### View 3: Software capture (bottom-up, UK-first scenarios)

| Segment | Buyer count assumption | ACV assumption | Revenue band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SME bid decision SaaS | 2,000 to 5,000 accounts | GBP 3,000 to GBP 12,000 | GBP 6M to GBP 60M |
| Institutional subscriptions | 100 to 300 accounts | GBP 25,000 to GBP 150,000 | GBP 2.5M to GBP 45M |
| Forensic/legal intelligence work | 50 to 200 matters per year | GBP 5,000 to GBP 40,000 per matter | GBP 0.25M to GBP 8M |

Sizing logic:
- Start with SME velocity, then move up ACV curve with institutional and forensic packages built on the same graph and decision logs.

## 5) Hidden Dynamics and Underserved Segments

### Underserved segments (minimum 5)

1. SME contractors needing deterministic go/no-go decisions, not more alerts.
2. Public law and investigative teams needing procurement-native entity linkage.
3. Mid-market suppliers needing both opportunity prioritization and supplier-risk context.
4. Public accountability organizations needing explainable, replayable AI outputs.
5. Authorities needing low-friction supplier-risk overlays without full enterprise transformation.
6. Advisors and bid consultancies that need repeatable decision systems across many clients.

### Non-obvious intersections (minimum 3)

1. Debarment + performance notices + ownership links create a new supplier-risk signal stack that most bid tools ignore.
2. Explainability is not just UX; it is a defensibility requirement for audits, legal challenge, and institutional trust.
3. The same entity-resolution layer can power SME commercialization, forensic investigations, and institutional monitoring with different surface workflows.
4. Pipeline notices create forward-looking relationship alpha long before notice-stage competitors react.

## 9) Final Recommendation and 12-Month North Star

### Best entry wedge (locked)

Launch as:
"Decision intelligence for public procurement (SME-first), not a tender alerts product."

### Second wedge

Expand into:
"Institutional supplier-risk and procurement assurance intelligence."

### Moat compounding path

Build and protect:
- normalized procurement data graph,
- entity linkage and confidence,
- decision event logs with reasons and risk flags,
- outcome feedback loops (bid, win, loss, conversion).

### 12-month strategic north star

By month 12, ProcessEd should be able to prove:

1. SME revenue engine repeatability (pilot to paid conversion with stable unit economics).
2. Institutional-ready risk overlays (supplier-performance, debarment, ownership context).
3. Differentiated procurement truth graph with explainability and provenance as standard output.

If these three are achieved, ProcessEd transitions from "tool" to "category infrastructure."
