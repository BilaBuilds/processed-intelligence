# Netherlands Demo Script

Status: demo script for sample Netherlands procurement intelligence. The opportunity data shown is fixture/demo data, not verified live coverage.

## 60-Second Script

I am testing a Netherlands expansion for ProcessEd Intelligence, focused on construction, civils, water, roads, maintenance, and public works.

The idea is simple: take Dutch and EU procurement sources such as TenderNed and TED, normalize them into an English commercial view, then rank opportunities by fit, urgency, buyer signal, and recommended action.

This dashboard is a sample demo, not live verified coverage yet. It shows the intended workflow: Netherlands-specific opportunities, EU/cross-border opportunities, source coverage, outreach segments, and opportunity cards with a clear next step.

What I want to learn is whether this would save time for bid, commercial, and market-entry teams. I am looking for feedback on source coverage, ranking logic, and whether a four-week pilot around specific CPVs or buyer categories would be useful.

## 3-Minute Script

I am going to show a Netherlands procurement intelligence demo for ProcessEd. This is deliberately a sample/demo pack. It is not claiming verified live coverage yet, and every record is labelled that way.

The expansion thesis is that Dutch and EU procurement is a practical next market for ProcessEd because infrastructure demand is visible across TenderNed, TED/EU notices, and buyer categories such as municipalities, provinces, Rijkswaterstaat, water authorities, ports, and transport bodies.

The first view is the dashboard summary. It shows the total sample opportunities, the split between Netherlands-specific and EU/cross-border examples, high-urgency items, and average fit score. The point is not the exact numbers; the point is the review workflow.

The opportunity cards show how a commercial team would work through the feed. Each card has a buyer, region, category, deadline, value estimate, fit score, urgency, and recommended action. For example, a water authority or municipal drainage item should lead to a different qualification action than a cross-border TED framework.

The buyer signal field is important. ProcessEd is not just listing tenders. It is trying to explain why a buyer matters: recurring public works demand, infrastructure asset ownership, water resilience need, or cross-border procurement visibility.

The enriched version also shows how Gemini Flash can add short qualification notes to help a bid or sales team decide whether to qualify, partner, monitor, or ignore. Those notes still sit inside the sample/demo boundary and should not be treated as verified facts.

The source coverage section is where I want feedback. Which sources are essential? Which CPV groups are too broad? Which Dutch buyer categories matter most? And what would make the output trusted enough for a weekly pilot?

The practical ask is a short feedback or source-validation conversation. If the workflow looks useful, the next step is a four-week pilot around a narrow segment such as water resilience, roads maintenance, public works frameworks, or UK/DACH firms exploring Netherlands entry.
