# DACH AI Governance GTM Note

## 1. Executive Summary

This is a secondary expansion thesis for a separate AI governance / agentic AI audit advisory offer. It is not the main ProcessEd procurement dashboard product, and it should not distract from the current UK/NL Hostinger procurement dashboard pilot execution.

Positioning:

> AI governance routing and deployment-readiness for companies adopting agentic AI before works council, regulator, or internal risk teams block deployment.

Market entry priority:

1. Germany first.
2. Switzerland second.
3. Austria as a low-priority German-language extension only.

Verified facts:

- The current ProcessEd priority is the Hostinger procurement dashboard and pilot outreach.
- This DACH note is for a separate AI governance / multi-agent audit advisory offer.
- Public use of any regulatory or market-specific claim needs source verification before publishing.

Assumptions to validate:

- German mid-market and enterprise teams will feel deployment friction from works councils, risk teams, and AI Act implementation uncertainty.
- Swiss regulated and reputation-sensitive firms will pay for third-party AI risk, outsourcing, and deployment-readiness reviews.
- Austria is not large or urgent enough to justify separate GTM motion before Germany is validated.

## 2. Why Germany First

Germany is the first DACH market to test because the likely friction is operational, not only legal. Companies adopting agentic AI may need to route use cases through works councils, data protection, compliance, security, procurement, and line-of-business owners before deployment.

Germany hook:

- Works council friction.
- Multi-regulator routing.
- AI Act implementation uncertainty.
- Internal classification and routing gaps.
- Conservative enterprise deployment culture.

The commercial wedge is not generic EU AI Act compliance. The wedge is helping teams classify, route, evidence, and prepare agentic AI deployments so internal stakeholders can approve or block with clarity.

## 3. Germany Positioning Thesis

Core thesis:

German companies adopting agentic AI will hit internal approval bottlenecks before they hit technical bottlenecks. The pain is likely to show up as unclear ownership, works council questions, data protection concerns, vendor risk reviews, and uncertainty about whether a use case is low-risk, high-risk, employee-facing, customer-facing, or operationally sensitive.

Primary message:

"Before your agentic AI deployment stalls in works council, compliance, security, or risk review, we map the use case, classify the deployment, identify routing owners, and produce a deployment-readiness evidence pack."

What to avoid:

- Do not sell generic EU AI Act compliance.
- Do not pretend to provide legal advice.
- Do not claim the offer guarantees approval.
- Do not claim German clients or revenue.

## 4. Switzerland Positioning Thesis

Switzerland is second priority. The likely wedge is not works council friction first; it is risk governance, outsourcing, third-party AI exposure, auditability, and reputational risk.

Switzerland hook:

- FINMA expectations for regulated entities.
- Outsourcing and third-party AI risk.
- Vendor governance and operational resilience.
- Reputational risk from poorly controlled AI deployments.

Primary message:

"For Swiss regulated or reputation-sensitive firms, we review agentic AI use cases for governance routing, third-party risk, evidence gaps, and deployment-readiness before internal risk or audit teams escalate concerns."

Claims needing verification before public use:

- Any specific FINMA circular, guidance, or enforcement interpretation.
- Any sector-specific obligations for banks, insurers, asset managers, or fintechs.
- Any claim that Swiss firms are actively budgeting for this category.

## 5. Austria Positioning Thesis

Austria should be treated as a German-language extension only, not a separate priority yet.

Austria hook:

- Similar German-language messaging.
- EU AI Act context.
- Regional firms that already operate across Germany/Austria.

Current thesis:

Do not create a separate Austria GTM motion until Germany produces evidence of demand, repeatable objections, and a paid advisory path.

## 6. ICPs

Primary Germany ICPs:

- Mid-market and enterprise firms deploying agentic AI internally.
- Regulated or risk-sensitive sectors: financial services, insurance, healthcare-adjacent, industrials, logistics, manufacturing, energy, and critical suppliers.
- Firms with works council processes and employee-facing AI use cases.
- Companies where AI deployment involves multiple owners: legal, HR, compliance, security, procurement, IT, and operations.

Primary Switzerland ICPs:

- Banks, insurers, fintechs, asset managers, and regulated service providers.
- Firms adopting AI through vendors, outsourcing partners, or third-party platforms.
- Companies where reputational risk is a material blocker.

Buyer personas:

- Head of AI / AI Transformation Lead.
- Chief Risk Officer or operational risk lead.
- Data Protection Officer.
- Legal / Compliance Lead.
- Internal Audit Lead.
- Security / GRC Lead.
- HR Transformation Lead for employee-facing AI tools.

## 7. Offer Packaging

Package 1: Agentic AI Deployment Readiness Review

- 2-week review of 1-3 agentic AI use cases.
- Use-case classification.
- Stakeholder routing map.
- Risk and evidence gap register.
- Deployment-readiness scorecard.
- Works council / risk-team question pack.
- Executive summary with go / pause / remediate recommendation.

Package 2: Multi-Agent Governance Routing Workshop

- Half-day or one-day workshop.
- Maps approval owners and decision points.
- Defines use-case intake questions.
- Creates a first-pass routing matrix.
- Produces a practical governance workflow for agentic AI deployments.

Package 3: Vendor And Third-Party AI Risk Review

- Focused review of AI vendor or agentic workflow.
- Data flow, human oversight, auditability, security, and accountability checks.
- Third-party risk questions for procurement, legal, and risk teams.

## 8. Pricing Hypothesis

These are hypotheses only, not achieved pricing and not revenue forecasts.

- Entry readiness review: EUR 4,000-8,000.
- Workshop: EUR 2,500-6,000.
- Regulated-sector vendor risk review: EUR 6,000-15,000.
- Monthly advisory retainer after validation: EUR 2,000-5,000.

Assumptions to validate:

- Buyers see enough urgency to pay for a short diagnostic.
- The strongest early buyer is risk/compliance/AI transformation, not procurement.
- A fixed-scope readiness review is easier to sell than a broad governance transformation project.

## 9. 90-Day Validation Plan

Days 1-15:

- Keep ProcessEd Hostinger procurement pilot execution as the primary focus.
- Build a private DACH prospect list of 50 Germany-first accounts.
- Verify Germany-specific claims with credible sources before public use.
- Draft a one-page readiness review offer.

Days 16-45:

- Run 15-20 discovery conversations.
- Test three angles: works council friction, regulator/risk routing, and internal classification gaps.
- Track objections and buying triggers.
- Do not promise legal compliance or guaranteed approval.

Days 46-75:

- Offer 3 unpaid or low-cost diagnostic calls only if they produce strong learning.
- Try to convert 1-2 fixed-scope paid readiness reviews.
- Build a sample anonymised output pack using dummy use cases.

Days 76-90:

- Decide whether Germany deserves active GTM.
- If Germany shows weak urgency, pause DACH and return attention fully to UK/NL ProcessEd execution.
- If Germany shows strong pull, test Switzerland with a narrower FINMA / third-party risk message.

## 12. Decision Gate: When To Actually Enter Germany

Enter Germany only if at least three of these are true:

- 10 or more qualified discovery calls confirm the same deployment-readiness pain.
- At least 3 prospects ask for a concrete readiness review or workshop proposal.
- At least 1 prospect is willing to pay for a fixed-scope diagnostic.
- Works council, risk routing, or internal classification gaps appear repeatedly.
- The offer can be delivered without pulling focus from the ProcessEd Hostinger procurement pilot.
- Public claims have been verified and separated from assumptions.

Do not enter Germany yet if:

- The UK/NL ProcessEd pilot requires execution focus.
- Interest is only generic AI curiosity.
- Prospects only want free legal interpretation of the EU AI Act.
- There is no budget owner.
- The offer cannot be described without unsupported claims.
